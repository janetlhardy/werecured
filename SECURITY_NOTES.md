# Staying safe — scams aimed at the shop

_Kept off the public website._

## The pattern that keeps turning up

In August 2026 someone emailed claiming to be a fellow store owner wanting to refer customers.
It was a scam, and it ran to a standard script:

1. **Friendly, flattering, and completely vague** — no store name, no website, no named product,
   no signature. Real buyers and promoters are specific, because specifics can be checked.
2. **A sad story about another store closing without refunds** — builds instant rapport, and quietly
   makes "refunds and payment problems" a normal topic for later.
3. **A small unverifiable favour first** — "I'll send your link to my customers" — to create a sense
   of obligation before asking for anything.
4. **Push to move to WhatsApp or Instagram**, with a holiday as the excuse for why email won't work.

That last step is the giveaway. **If someone has signal for WhatsApp, they have signal for email.**
There is no holiday where Instagram works and Gmail doesn't. The real goal is a personal phone
number and a conversation with no record, no spam filter and nobody to report to.

## Rules

- **Keep it in email.** "I don't use WhatsApp for business" is a complete answer. Genuine contacts
  shrug; scammers push, and the push is the answer.
- **Never click a link in an unsolicited email, and never type the Shopify password into anything
  reached from one.** Always get to Shopify by bookmark or by typing the address. The Shopify login
  is the crown jewels — shop, payouts and customer data.
- **Every sale goes through the Shopify checkout.** Never cheque, transfer, or "my agent will pay".
- **Never refund an overpayment.** Overpay-then-refund is the oldest one there is; the original
  payment bounces afterwards.
- **Never pay a fee** to someone who approached first — for promotion, listing, or "insurance".
- **Don't share** phone number, home address, or banking details.
- **Ship only after payment has genuinely cleared**, not on a payment confirmation email.

## If in doubt

Ask one question: *"What's your store's name and website?"* Genuine people answer instantly.
Then block, delete, and don't reply — replying at all confirms the address is live.
