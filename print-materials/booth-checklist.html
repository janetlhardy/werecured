Show & Booth Checklist

Everything learned from the November 2026 gift show, in one place. The big idea: show less, light it well, and let the colours glow against black. Space around a piece tells people it's art.

Before you book a show

  • Ask the organiser how many people came last year and what sort of things sold.
  • Ask what the other vendors sell. Lots of food, kits and resale = a bargain crowd.
  • Ask other vendors how it went for them — they'll usually be honest.
  • Visit as a shopper first if you can. Look at the lighting, and whether people are carrying bags out.
  • Ask if booth fabric has to be flame-retardant.
  • Ask if there's power at the booth (and whether it costs extra), and how far the outlet is.
  • Ask for a booth on a busy aisle or a corner if they let you choose.

To buy or gather

  • Black matte fabric for behind each rack — black queen flat sheets work. Flame-retardant if the show asks.
  • Big binder clips or bulldog clips and zip ties (plus scissors to cut the ties).
  • Black matte tablecloth that reaches the floor — not satin, not white.
  • 3–4 warm LED clip-on lights (not white work lamps), an extension cord and a power bar.
  • Battery LED puck lights to tuck under or behind see-through pieces so they glow.
  • LED light pad (tracing / diamond-painting kind) to backlight a few pendants.
  • 5–6 empty wine bottles — a mix of colours. Soak the labels off, or keep pretty ones.
  • 2–3 more busts or necklace stands at different heights.
  • Plate stands and small risers so flat pieces stand up instead of lying down.
  • A small mirror for trying on pendants.
  • Plastic bins that fit under the table for back stock.
  • Coloured dot stickers (green, blue, yellow) for the bottoms of the wine stoppers.
  • UV flashlight and a small dark box — “peek inside to see them glow!” for the Monster Splats.

Packing the stock

  • Pick 3–5 “hero” pieces — the showstoppers that pull people in from the aisle.
  • Bring about half of everything, and plan to put out only a third of that.
  • One of each coaster style on display, the rest boxed underneath.
  • Choose 20–30 pendants to display, the rest in a box to refill from.
  • Pack the Monster Splats — they were the biggest crowd-pleaser.
  • Signs: QR table sign, Spilled sign, wine stopper sign, and an “All pendants $50” sign.
  • Business cards, bags, tissue, gift tags, tape, pen, notebook.
  • Phone charged, charger, card reader, cash float.

Setting up

  • Table at the front or along one side (an L-shape), so people can browse from the aisle without stepping in.
  • Jewellery closest to the aisle — small, detailed pieces need to be up close.
  • Black fabric behind every rack, reaching the floor to hide back stock.
  • Heroes at eye level, with space around each one and a light on it.
  • Group by colour or theme — blues and oceans together, autumn and sunflowers together, pinks together.
  • Leave gaps on every shelf. If a shelf looks full, take something off.
  • Nothing on the floor. Anything at foot level looks like overflow.
  • Pendants hanging from the resin hands, a few on busts, a few backlit on the light pad.
  • Wine stoppers: a few in bottles, the rest in one of your own bowls with the sign beside them.
  • Puck lights under the see-through pieces, clip lights aimed at the shelves.
  • Price on everything, and signs where one price covers a group.
  • Walk out into the aisle and look from 10 feet away. What catches your eye first? Is it a hero piece?
  • Take booth photos before the doors open.

During the show

  • Refill from the bins as things sell — keep the gaps.
  • Stand, and say hello to anyone who stops.
  • Jot down what people pick up and anything they say about prices.
  • Hand a card to everyone who lingers — “we ship anywhere in Canada.”

Afterwards

  • List what sold, to whom, and at what price.
  • Note what got the most attention, even if it didn't sell.
  • Post the crowd favourites on Instagram so people who took a card can find them again.
  • Send Claude the booth photos and your notes to update this list.
Remember: one quiet show doesn't mean the work or the prices are wrong. The right room matters more than anything on this list.
