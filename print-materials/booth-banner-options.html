Booth banner — three options to choose from

All three are 72″ × 24″ (6ft × 2ft), shown here scaled down. The dashed line is the safe area — nothing important crosses it, so hems and grommets can't eat any text.

The medallions design is gone. Option 1 is unchanged. Options 2 and 3 are both the all-capital Bungee version that matches the website — one on the photo, one on a light background, because 6 feet of near-black is a lot of dark to hang in a hall.

No QR code on the banner, on purpose — a code hanging behind your table is too far away for phones to read. The QR lives on the table sign (table-sign.html), where people can reach it.

Pick one, then tell Claude which — the others get deleted and the winner becomes booth-banner.html.

To make the print file: File → Print → Destination Save as PDF → Paper size 72 × 24 in (or “Custom”) → Margins None → tick Background graphics → Save. If the printer asks for bleed, tell them to add 0.25″ each side — there's plenty of colour to pull from.

Photo note — Options 1 and 2 need a bigger photo file. The copy of IMG_3091.jpeg in the website folder is 1384 × 1500 pixels. That is plenty for a business card, but spread across 6 feet — and now zoomed to 130% to keep the bare table out of shot — it works out to about 15 dots per inch. It will look soft close up.

The fix: open the original in Photos, choose File → Export → Export Unmodified Original, and give Claude the file. Straight off your phone it is likely around 3000 pixels wide, which lands at roughly 32 dots per inch here. That is low by desk-printing standards but normal for large-format vinyl — banner printers commonly work to artwork supplied at a tenth of final size at 300 dpi, which is the same 30 dpi. From more than a few feet away it reads as sharp.

Option 3 has no photo at all, so it prints perfectly sharp exactly as it stands.
